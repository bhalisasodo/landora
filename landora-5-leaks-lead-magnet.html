Before you start

Most local businesses don't have a traffic problem. They have a leaking bucket.

People already find you — through Google, Instagram, a friend's tag, a flyer at the till. The problem shows up one step later: on the page they land on, in the form they abandon, on the phone they never picked up. That's a leak. And it's costing you bookings you already paid to earn.

This isn't a sales brochure. It's the same five-point check Landora runs on every business before recommending a single thing. Go through each leak, do the 60-second check, and be honest with yourself — the goal is an accurate picture, not a passing grade.

1

The Dead End

Someone lands on your page ready to book — and there's nothing telling them what to do next.

The 60-second check

Open your own website on your phone, on data, like a stranger would. Scroll to the bottom of the first screen. Is there a button telling you exactly what to do — Book Now, Call Us, WhatsApp Us? Or just a logo and a paragraph about your history?

What it costs you

A visitor gives you a few seconds of attention. If nothing tells them what to do with it, they don't dig for it — they leave and try the next result on Google.

The fix

One clear action, repeated every scroll or two, in words that say exactly what happens next — not "Learn More," but "Book Your Slot" or "Get a Quote."

2

The Scavenger Hunt

Your price, hours, or location exist somewhere — buried three menus and a PDF deep.

The 60-second check

Time yourself finding your own prices or opening hours from the homepage. If it takes more than two taps, or you had to open a downloaded PDF to find it, that's the leak.

What it costs you

People don't message to ask "how much?" anymore — they leave and check a competitor who simply shows the number.

The fix

Put the basics — price, hours, location — where they land, not where they have to search. Nothing that matters should live more than one tap deep.

3

The Ghost Form

Someone fills in your enquiry form — and hears nothing back for days, if ever.

The 60-second check

Submit your own contact form right now. Time how long until you get any confirmation — an email, an SMS, anything. Then ask: who actually checks that inbox, and how often?

What it costs you

The person who filled in your form is comparing three businesses at once. Whoever replies first usually wins the booking — regardless of who's better.

The fix

An instant confirmation the moment someone submits, and a same-day human reply. If that's not realistic yet, route enquiries straight to WhatsApp instead of a form nobody checks.

4

The Mismatch

Your Instagram looks premium. Your website looks like it's from a different, cheaper business.

The 60-second check

Open your Instagram or Facebook profile in one tab and your website in the other. Would a stranger believe these belong to the same business?

What it costs you

A gap between your social presence and your site reads as a trust problem, not a style choice — and trust is exactly what makes someone click "book" instead of "keep scrolling."

The fix

Carry the same fonts, colours, and photography from your socials onto your site. It should feel like one business walked through the door, not two.

5

The Desktop Trap

It looks great on a laptop. Almost nobody who finds you is using one.

The 60-second check

On your phone, check whether text overflows the screen, buttons are too small to tap accurately, or you have to pinch and zoom to read anything.

What it costs you

The large majority of local searches happen on a phone. A site that fights the visitor on mobile loses them before they even reach your offer.

The fix

Design for the phone screen first, not last. Buttons a thumb can hit, text that never needs zooming, forms with as few fields as possible.
