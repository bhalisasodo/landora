# Landora Repositioning Brief
### From landing-page specialist to full digital marketing studio

---

## 1. The Pivot, In One Line

Landora stops being "the landing page people" and becomes the studio that plugs sales pipeline leaks — wherever they happen. Websites don't disappear; they demote from *the product* to *one of the fixes*.

Brand voice, identity, and tone are unchanged. This is a scope change, not a rebrand. Sharp Mischief stays: confident, witty, plain-spoken, no gimmicks. The gremlin stays a knowing grin, not a mascot.

---

## 2. Why "The Leak / The Fix" Still Works

The original metaphor was built narrow — a leak was always "your funnel doesn't have a page that closes." That's actually a gift here, not a constraint: a leak is just *any point where a lead should convert and doesn't.* The frame was never really about websites. It was about lost revenue. That generalizes cleanly:

| Leak type | Where it happens | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dead End | No page that closes | Website |
| Ghost Traffic | Nobody sees you | Ads |
| Buried Alive | Invisible in search | SEO |
| Cold Trail | No presence between visits | Content/Social |
| Scavenger Hunt | Confusing journey across all of the above | Website + whichever channel is broken |

Same "leak" vocabulary, four surfaces instead of one. Nothing about the copy voice has to change — only the *scope of what counts as a leak.*

---

## 3. Service Menu

**Core (the diagnosis, always first):**
- Funnel/Leak Audit — where a prospective client's actual leak is, across site + ads + SEO + content. This becomes the entry point for every engagement, replacing the old single-SKU landing page pitch.

**Services (sold individually or bundled based on the audit):**
1. **Websites** — the original offer, now framed as one possible fix, not the default one. High-conversion landing pages and sites, same premium production bar.
2. **Ads** — paid acquisition (Meta/Google, whichever fits the client). Sold as "stop leaking traffic you're already paying for" as much as "get more traffic."
3. **SEO** — organic visibility. Positioned as the slow leak: costs nothing per click but bleeds opportunity daily if ignored.
4. **Content/Social** — the "between-visit" presence. Positioned as what keeps a fixed leak from reopening — retention/re-engagement, not just top-of-funnel noise.

**Why lead with the audit, not the service list:** a client naming their own service ("I need a website") is often misdiagnosing their own leak. Selling the audit first protects Landora from being commissioned to build the wrong fix — and it's a much better opener than "here are our four services," which reads like every other agency.

## 4. Updated Positioning Language

**Old tagline (landing pages only):**
> "One link that turns lookers into bookers."

**Updated tagline (studio-wide):**
> "We find where you're leaking. Then we fix it."

Keep "lookers into bookers" alive as a subhead or a website-specific line when the fix *is* a website — it's too good to bury, it just can't be the master line anymore since it's website-specific.

**Updated subhead (replaces "You don't need a prettier website...")**
> "You don't need more marketing. You need the leak found."

This does the same job the old subhead did — undercutting the obvious assumption (prettier website / more marketing) with the sharper truth (it's a diagnosis problem, not a production problem) — just widened past websites.

## 6. Pricing Model Shift

A single flat price worked for one productized SKU. A multi-service studio needs a model that doesn't force every client into the same box. Two options, not mutually exclusive:

**Option A — Audit as a paid entry point**
- Flat, low-friction price for the Leak Audit (keeps the old "no-brainer flat price" instinct alive, just moved to the diagnosis instead of the build)
- Custom quote for whatever fix(es) the audit recommends

**Option B — Tiered retainers post-audit**
- e.g. "Single Fix" (one service, project-based) vs. "Full Studio" (ongoing multi-service retainer)
- Website builds can stay closer to the old flat/near-flat pricing since that's already validated; Ads/SEO/Content are typically retainer-shaped by nature (ongoing spend management, ongoing content cadence) so forcing them into a one-time flat price will undersell the work

Recommendation: lead with Option A on the site (cheap, clear, no-brainer — matches the existing "no fluff" voice), and handle Option B in the sales conversation once the audit defines scope. Keeps the public-facing pricing section simple instead of a confusing 4-service price grid.
